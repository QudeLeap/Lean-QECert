Coding theory / Theorem

Stabilizer syndrome map laws

Theorem statement

Let $J$ be a finite set, let $M$ be a $J$-indexed family of binary Pauli generators for a stabilizer $S$, and define the generator-indexed syndrome $f_M(E)\in\mathbb{F}_2^J$ by declaring $f_M(E)(j)=0$ exactly when $E$ commutes with the generator at $j\in J$, and $f_M(E)(j)=1$ exactly when it anticommutes. For all binary Pauli errors $E,A,B$, the laws are $f_M(E)=0\Longleftrightarrow E\in N(S)$ and $f_M(A)=f_M(B)\Longleftrightarrow f_M(AB)=0$. Moreover, the source product $AB$ and the relative product $A^\dagger B$ differ only by a binary global phase, so $f_M(AB)=f_M(A^\dagger B)$. Thus the second equivalence is unchanged when written with the relative product.
