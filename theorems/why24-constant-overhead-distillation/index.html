WHY24 constant-overhead CCZ distillation

Theorem statement

Fix $\mathbb{F}_{1024}$ with the selected self-dual binary basis and the induced computational-basis identification of one qudit with ten qubits. Let $U_1^{(7)}$ be the resulting non-Clifford third-level diagonal gate. There are constants $\Omega>0$ and $p_{\mathrm{th}}>0$ and an explicit infinite family of one-round, no-postselection stabilizer protocols based on weighted triorthogonal algebraic-geometry codes over $\mathbb{F}_{1024}$. For every target error $\epsilon>0$ and every physical rate $0\leq p_{\mathrm{ph}}<p_{\mathrm{th}}$, there is an index $j_0=j_0(\epsilon,p_{\mathrm{ph}})$ such that every sufficiently large family member $j\ge j_0$ maps $\zeta_j=70n_j$ noisy $CCZ$ input states to $k_j$ output $CCZ$ states. Provided their preparations obey a local-stochastic model with a random fault set, arbitrary correlated CPTP maps supported at faulty locations, and the product-tail condition at that rate, the polynomial-time dual-AG decoder corrects Z-type qudit errors through radius $t_j$, the output error is at most $\epsilon$, and $\zeta_j/k_j\leq\Omega$. The selected basis admits a finite 70-CCZ resource preparation and conversion. The same family admits a catalytic $CCZ$-and-$T$ conversion to three $T$ states; its separately prepared $T$ seed is amortized only over a growing batch of outputs.
